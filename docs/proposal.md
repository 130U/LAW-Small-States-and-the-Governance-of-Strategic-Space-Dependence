# The proposal as a working instrument

Part VI of the Article proposes a decision standard of **accountable acceptance** and translates it into licensing
and procurement terms. This page sets the standard out as a checklist that an authority can use before it commits
to an essential space-dependent function. Every element comes from the Article's text.

## 1. Map the entitlements

Control over a space system is a bundle of five entitlements. The first step is to write down who holds each one.

| Entitlement | Question | Holder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permission | Who can allow or refuse the activity in advance? | |
| Title | Who owns the asset? | |
| Operation | Who commands the system in use? | |
| Interruption and priority | Who can stop the service, or claim it first? Which control segment, export license or ground station does that power run through? | |
| Voice | Who takes part in the rules that govern everyone? | |

The Article's three cases, mapped this way:

| Entitlement | New Zealand (launch regulation) | Norway (ASBM) | Luxembourg (LUXEOSys) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Permission | Held, and exercised through conditions on every permit | Not at issue: the state owns the operator | Not at issue: a state program |
| Title | Not held: the payloads belong to foreign operators | Held: Space Norway owns the platforms | Held |
| Operation | Not held | Platform command held; the American and commercial payloads are separate | Contracted to a third-party provider, with antennas on rented infrastructure abroad |
| Interruption and priority | Before launch only | Not resolved by the published terms | Not documented in the audit |

## 2. Accountable-acceptance review

Completed for each **specified public function**, for example emergency communications for the coast guard,
Earth-observation tasking for disaster response, or launch licensing for a class of payloads.

| # | Question | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Interruption.** Who can interrupt or materially change this function: a supplier, a foreign government, a host-state regulator, an adversary? | |
| 2 | **Response rights.** What information and what contractual or legal rights permit a response: notice, consultation, operational data, transition? | |
| 3 | **Funded capacity.** What funded capability makes that response feasible: staff, a ground segment, an alternative service, a terrestrial substitute? | |
| 4 | **Material gaps.** Where do 1 to 3 fall short? | |
| 5 | **Decision.** Accept the gap with reasons, or revise the arrangement? Name the public decision maker. | |
| 6 | **Scrutiny.** Does the importance of the function warrant legislative or independent review? Where disclosure would defeat the purpose, who provides confidential review? | |

**The kind of duty.** The review is procedural in the way environmental review is procedural. It forbids
uninformed dependence and leaves the choice to depend with the responsible authority. A duty to identify and
explain is a burden on the buyer's own process, and it imposes on the seller only what the buyer then chooses to
negotiate.

**Proportionality.** Requirements scale with the importance of the function, the consequences of interruption and
the feasibility of replacement. A government can reasonably accept a supplier's suspension power for a service
whose temporary loss is manageable, and require stronger notice, fallback capacity or confidential review for
emergency communications.

## 3. Licensing terms

For the authorization of a national activity. They apply the General Assembly's 2013 recommendations on national
space legislation to a risk those recommendations do not name, dependence on a foreign input.

| Term | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Responsible operators and relevant jurisdictions identified | Know whom to supervise and whose law also applies |
| Permitted uses stated | Regulate intended use, as New Zealand's payload principles do |
| Information the regulator can obtain and assess | Make continuing supervision possible after launch |
| Procedure for significant changes | Changes in use, operator or payload return to the regulator, including a transfer of ownership or control in orbit |
| Published reasons, at a level compatible with security and commercial interests | Accountability that does not defeat the purpose |
| Budget and technical competence for supervision, including access to outside expertise | Retained authority is usable only with the capacity to exercise it |

## 4. Procurement terms

For essential services from external suppliers. Their models are in force elsewhere.

| Term | Purpose | Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspension grounds | Know in advance what can stop the service | |
| Advance notice | Time to activate alternatives | Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 art. 30: termination rights and notice periods |
| Emergency consultation | A channel when interests diverge | |
| Access to necessary operational information | Enough to respond and to supervise | Directive 2009/81/EC art. 23: notice of changes in organization or supply chain |
| Disclosure of restrictions | Any limit on disclosure, transfer or use that arises from export-control or security arrangements | Directive 2009/81/EC art. 23 |
| Crisis capacity | A commitment to the capacity required to meet additional needs | Directive 2009/81/EC art. 23 |
| Transition | Terminals, data and operating practices that permit a move to another service | Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 arts. 28, 30: exit strategies and a mandatory adequate transition period |
| Compatibility with safety requirements, lawful sanctions and export controls | Terms a supplier can lawfully perform | |

## 5. Financial test

- Compare **feasible alternatives over the life of the system**: ownership, commercial service, pooled capability
  or a terrestrial substitute. No presumption favors national hardware.
- Include ground infrastructure, personnel, operation and contingencies. In LUXEOSys the title was acquired and
  the operation was bought separately.
- For shared systems, account for the public's financing risk, as Norway's equity commitment shows, and state the
  conditions of release, as the Storting did.
- Give parliament material revisions **while choices remain open**.

## 6. Three checks on the result

- **Redundancy is shown, not assumed.** Two suppliers that share gateways, export jurisdictions or other points of
  failure are one alternative.
- **Shared support needs governance.** Regional purchasing and shared expertise spread fixed costs when the shared
  institution is itself governed workably.
- **Collective limits still apply.** Domestic safeguards accompany restraint against debris and cross-border
  disruption.
