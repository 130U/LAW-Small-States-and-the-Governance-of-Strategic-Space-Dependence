# Where this Article sits

The debate about small states and space runs on two words, dependence and sovereignty, and three literatures stand
behind it. Small-state theory explains why a small state enters a dependent relationship. The literature on
network power explains how a chokepoint becomes leverage. Space law says who is responsible and who has
jurisdiction. None of them says how decisions are allocated inside a cooperative arrangement once it exists, which
is the part law can design. The Article supplies that account from property theory and the economics of incomplete
contracts, tests it against administrative records, and identifies the kind of legal duty that follows. This page
records what each body of authority settles, what it leaves open, and what the Article adds. The complete list of
186 authorities and sources is in the [bibliography](bibliography.md).

## What the Article adds

| Body of work | Where it stops | The Article's step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small-state theory: Long; Thorhallsson | It explains why a small state seeks shelter and how it gains influence | It explains how decisions are allocated inside the relationship |
| Network power: Farrell and Newman | Its cases are financial messaging and the internet | It carries the reasoning to satellite services and names the chokepoints: a control segment, an export license, a ground station |
| Property theory and incomplete contracts: Hohfeld; Honoré; Grossman, Hart and Moore | Ownership as a bundle of incidents; real ownership as the residual right to decide | Five entitlements for a space system, and the finding that the residual right in a crisis follows the chokepoint |
| Space law on national legislation | Authorization and continuing supervision | Licensing terms for a risk the guidance does not name |
| Procedural duties in administrative law: NEPA | A duty of informed decision for environmental effects | The same structure for dependence: accountable acceptance |

## Key authorities

### Concepts

<a id="hohfeld1913"></a>
**Wesley Newcomb Hohfeld, *Some Fundamental Legal Conceptions as Applied in Judicial Reasoning*, 23 Yale L.J. 16 (1913); A.M. Honoré, *Ownership*, *in* Oxford Essays in Jurisprudence 107 (A.G. Guest ed., 1961).**
Legal relations resolve into distinct entitlements, and ownership is a set of incidents, among them the rights to
possess, to use and to manage, and the right to the income. Nothing requires one person to hold them all. The Article reads control
over a space system the same way and separates permission, title, operation, interruption and priority, and voice.

<a id="grossman1986"></a>
**Sanford J. Grossman & Oliver D. Hart, *The Costs and Benefits of Ownership*, 94 J. Pol. Econ. 691 (1986); Oliver Hart & John Moore, *Property Rights and the Nature of the Firm*, 98 J. Pol. Econ. 1119 (1990).**
When contracts are incomplete, what makes an owner an owner is the residual right to decide in circumstances the
contract does not cover. The theory was built for firms. The Article applies it to a state's arrangements with
suppliers and partners and finds that the residual right in a crisis sits with whoever holds a necessary input.

<a id="farrell2019"></a>
**Henry Farrell & Abraham L. Newman, *Weaponized Interdependence*, 44 Int'l Sec. 42 (2019).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1162/isec_a_00351)
Position in a network, combined with jurisdiction over its hubs, gives a state coercive leverage. The cases are
financial messaging and the internet. The extension to satellite services is the Article's own, and the
entitlement map is what makes it specific.

<a id="thorhallsson2018"></a>
**Baldur Thorhallsson, *Studying Small States: A Review*, 1 Small States & Territories 17 (2018).**
Small states' alignments are the purchase of political, economic and societal shelter from larger powers. The
account explains entry into a dependent relationship. It is silent on the allocation of decisions inside it.

<a id="long2017"></a>
**Tom Long, *Small States, Great Power? Gaining Influence Through Intrinsic, Derivative, and Collective Power*, 19 Int'l Stud. Rev. 185 (2017).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/isr/viw040)
Small states draw influence from what they hold, from a patron and from institutions. The typology organizes the
three cases: territory for New Zealand, partnership for Norway, institutions for the multilateral record.

### Law

<a id="ost1967"></a>
**Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space, Jan. 27, 1967, 610 U.N.T.S. 205.**
Equal freedom of use, state responsibility with authorization and continuing supervision, and registry
jurisdiction. The treaty preserves rights and assigns responsibilities that can exceed a state's supervisory
reach, and it supplies neither access to another actor's systems nor continuity of a purchased service.

<a id="res6874"></a>
**G.A. Res. 68/74, Recommendations on National Legislation Relevant to the Peaceful Exploration and Use of Outer Space (Dec. 11, 2013).**
Space activities should require authorization by a competent national authority, with continuing supervision that
persists through a transfer of ownership or control in orbit. The licensing terms of Part VI apply these
recommendations to dependence on a foreign input, which they do not name.

<a id="usml"></a>
**22 C.F.R. § 121.1, Category XV(e)(17) and note 2.**
A commercial spacecraft remains subject to the Export Administration Regulations when it carries a hosted payload
that itself stays on the Munitions List. Platform and payload are different legal objects before any contract is
signed, which is the Norwegian arrangement stated as regulation.

<a id="robertson1989"></a>
**Robertson v. Methow Valley Citizens Council, 490 U.S. 332 (1989).** [opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/490/332/)
The National Environmental Policy Act prescribes the necessary process and does not mandate particular results;
it prohibits uninformed agency action, and leaves unwise action to the agency. Accountable acceptance is a duty of
this class, applied to dependence.

<a id="dora2022"></a>
**Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, arts. 28, 30, 2022 O.J. (L 333) 1; Directive 2009/81/EC, art. 23, 2009 O.J. (L 216) 76.**
Financial firms that rely on outside technology providers must have exit strategies and contract for termination
rights, notice and a transition period. Defense buyers may require disclosure of export-control restrictions, a
commitment to crisis capacity and notice of supply-chain changes. The clauses exist. What the Article adds is the
duty to explain their absence from an arrangement for an essential space function.

### Records

<a id="mbie2022"></a>
**New Zealand Ministry of Business, Innovation & Employment, Statutory Review of the Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act 2017 (2022).** [pdf](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/assets/report-on-the-statutory-review-of-the-oshaa-2017.pdf)
Seventy payload permits between December 2017 and April 2022, none declined, and ministerial conditions on every
permit and license. The same review records that remote-sensing products can be resold with no guarantee about
where they will end up. An official document states the distinction between permission and supervision.

<a id="igis2021"></a>
**Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security, Report into a Review of GCSB and NZSIS Activity and Assessments under the Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act 2017 (2021).**
An oversight review of how national-security assessments of payloads were made. It shows that a discretionary
permission is hard to apply consistently without agreed risk criteria.

<a id="innst330"></a>
**Stortinget, Innst. 330 S (2017-2018).** [text](https://stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Innstillinger/Stortinget/2017-2018/inns-201718-330s/?m=0)
The equity commitment to Space Norway was approved on the ministry's assessment that the project must meet
the armed forces' need for military broadband under Norwegian control, with no funds released until binding customer
agreements were concluded, and with the ground station and operations center in Norway. A legislature financed a
partnership on stated terms about who would control what.

<a id="cdc2022"></a>
**Cour des comptes du Grand-Duché de Luxembourg, Rapport spécial sur la gestion financière du projet LUXEOSys (2022).**
The authorized envelope rose from EUR170 million to EUR309 million, and operation of the system was to be
performed by a third-party provider at EUR75 million, with antennas on rented infrastructure in Belgium. Title
was acquired and the capacity to operate was bought separately.

<a id="ce2018"></a>
**Conseil d'État du Grand-Duché de Luxembourg, Avis n° 52.735 sur le projet de loi n° 7264 (July 17, 2018).**
The opinion regretted that the documentation gave no details of the split between investment and operating
expenditure. The gap in information was on the record before the commitment.

<a id="esa1975"></a>
**Convention for the Establishment of a European Space Agency, May 30, 1975, 1297 U.N.T.S. 161.**
Optional programs, one vote per member, and industrial return linked to contributions. Regional pooling
organizes shared capacity and allocates authority by contribution.
